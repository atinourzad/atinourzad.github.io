PhD student · The Ohio State University

Fatemeh NourzadI also go by Atena.

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at The Ohio State University, advised by Professor Ness B. Shroff. I study how AI systems can acquire new capabilities and adapt to different goals while preserving their reasoning and reliable behavior.

My interest in engineering began with childhood conversations with my father, an earthquake-engineering professor. I found my own path through electrical engineering, drawn to the probability and optimization behind communication networks. Reading about machine learning for network optimization led me toward ML itself.

During my master’s at the University of Tehran, I worked with the International Institute of Earthquake Engineering and Seismology to detect earthquakes in noisy recordings. That experience sparked an interest in reliability that I carried into safe reinforcement learning at Ohio State, then into language-model alignment.

In collaboration with researchers at Google, I studied how one language model can adapt to different human preferences without retraining. Through AI-EDGE, I also worked on distributed LLM training, bringing together my interests in learning algorithms and communication networks.

More recently, I have investigated how learning to use tools changes LLM agents’ reasoning, and developed methods to help them retain these abilities. I am now exploring recursive self-improvement in multi-agent systems, asking how agents can improve collectively while their underlying models remain fixed.

Research

Tool-using agents & reasoning retention

How does learning to use tools change an agent’s reasoning? We investigated how tool-use reinforcement learning reshapes reasoning, finding that it can shorten deliberation even when its effects on accuracy vary across tasks. We developed methods to help agents acquire tool-use skills while retaining their ability to reason independently.

Preference-conditioned & federated alignment

We study how language models can balance competing objectives, from adapting to individual preferences to learning collaboratively across clients.

Meta-learning for preference steering

In collaboration with researchers at Google, we developed a meta-learning framework that trains a single language-model policy to respond to different preference combinations at inference time, without retraining. We evaluated how well it balances competing objectives and generalizes to unseen preferences, and investigated optimization and geometric barriers that can limit continuous steering.

Federated multi-objective alignment

How can clients align a shared language model when their objectives conflict and their data remain local? We developed a federated multi-objective alignment framework in which clients solve regularized optimization problems locally. This controls cross-client objective disagreement and reduces communication by avoiding the transmission of separate gradients for every objective. We established finite-time Pareto-stationarity bounds and evaluated the method across heterogeneous clients.

Learning across distributed systems

Institutions have useful compute, but rarely share identical hardware or networks. Through AI-EDGE, I co-developed systems for pretraining and federated fine-tuning across geographically distributed GPU clusters.
